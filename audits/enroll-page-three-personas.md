# /pages/enroll, read three ways

The page names its own three buyers in the "Built for three women" section. This walks the
page as each of them, in reading order, and records where she stops trusting it.

Read after the schedule went in: Vision Room Tuesdays 12 PM ET, Shadow Labs every other
Wednesday 12 PM ET.

---

## What all three hit

### 1. The class is scheduled during work

The page's own targeting, in the `why` section:

> You're tired of asking off for **your own life**. You've got a "good job" that's
> **quietly draining you**.

The Vision Room meets **Tuesday at 12 PM ET**.

That is the middle of a shift. The page is aimed at employed women and schedules its live
session inside employment, and it never says a word about it. "Every session is recorded"
answers the learning question. It does not answer *"I am buying a live program I cannot
attend live."*

This is not necessarily a scheduling problem. Noon may be exactly right for the women who
already lash. But the page has to pick a story and tell it: either the recordings are a
first-class path, or the buyer is expected to be free at noon. Saying nothing lets her
assume the worst.

### 2. "250+ Women Trained" is on the page four times

- Marquee, twice
- Animated stat counter: `250+ / Women Trained`, `1,000+ / Clients Served`, `9+ / Years of Artistry`
- Founder paragraph: "trained over 250 women, and served 1,000+ clients"

Plus: "Trusted by clients including celebrities like Summer Walker."

This is the claim that came out of the application form under the no-fabricated-proof rule.
If the numbers are real and documented, they belong here and they are strong. If any is an
estimate, it is the most exposed thing on the page, and the buyer most likely to check is
the one this page most needs to convince.

`9+ Years of Artistry` also does not match the verified timeline. Actively lashing from 2018
is eight years in 2026.

## The Beginner

> "You're just getting into the industry, or you've wanted this for years and keep waiting
> to feel ready."

**Working for her:** the headline is aimed straight at her. She is named first in the three
women. "Do I need any experience? None." The whole "how do I learn this online" section. The
Performance Guarantee.

### Where she stalls

**The model is a requirement with no answer.** The page says she does a full set on a real
person before her first paying client. It never says who that person is. Does she find her?
Is it a friend? Does she pay her? What if nobody will let a beginner near their eyes? For
someone at zero this is the most practical fear on the page, and the page raises it and walks
away. One sentence fixes it.

**Licensing is FAQ number eight of ten.** It sits below "Can I split the payment." For a
beginner, whether she can legally take a paying client is a top-three question, and in some
states the answer materially changes what she is buying. Her own blog post on state
requirements earns 86 organic sessions a quarter with no CTA on it, which is proof this is
what beginners search for before they buy.

**She cannot tell what she needs to already own.** Equipped CEO ships a Complete Professional
Studio. So what does a Certified CEO beginner practice on? A bed? A chair? A lamp? That gap
is the entire $1,000 between the tiers and it is only ever described from the Equipped side.
She has to infer the absence.

**Tuesday noon lands hardest on her**, because she is the one most likely still working the
job the page told her is draining her.

**Small mismatch:** the `why` section says "Right now you might be charging $75. Maybe $150."
A true beginner charges nothing. Two sentences after being welcomed, the page is talking past
her.

## The Stalled Pro

> "You're trained. Maybe even certified. But your chair is still empty... **You don't need
> more technique.** You need the part nobody taught you."

**Working for her:** Method pillars 04, 05 and 06 are hers exactly. Week 5 and Week 6.
Courtney's testimonial. Lash Cash Q4 is built for her.

### The biggest mismatch on the page

**She is told she does not need technique, then sold four weeks of technique.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | Foundations |
| Week 2 | Core Technique |
| Week 3 | Advanced Artistry |
| Week 4 | Refinement |
| Week 5 | The Business |
| Week 6 | Launch |

Four of six Vision Rooms are the thing the page just told her she does not need. Two are
what she came for. She is paying $2,997 for two sessions, and the page never resolves the
contradiction it created one screen earlier.

There is a real answer available: if her chair is empty, her retention may be part of why,
and weeks 1 to 4 are where that gets found and fixed. That is a good argument. **The page
does not make it.** It just puts the two claims near each other and hopes she does not
notice.

**Lash Cash Q4 is her actual product, sold as an accessory.** Entity, website, back end, AI,
getting clients, Q4 plan. That is the Stalled Pro's curriculum, start to finish, for $997
standalone. The page offers it to her as a $497 bolt-on to a program she half needs, or under
"Everyone else, $997," framed as the leftover option rather than as her path. For a genuinely
trained artist, Lash Cash alone is often the honest recommendation, and the page will not say
so.

**"Most Chosen" sits on Equipped**, whose headline differentiator is the Complete Professional
Studio. She already has a studio. Shadow Labs and the 1:1s genuinely are for her, but they are
bullets three and four behind furniture she does not need.

**"Product for your first 30 clients"** frames the kit as beginner equipment. She has had
clients and probably has vendors.

## The Burned Buyer

> "You already paid for a course. You got a certificate, a kit you had to figure out by
> yourself, and nobody answering once the money cleared. **You're scared to spend again.**"

She does not read the page. She audits it.

**Working for her:** the Performance Guarantee is specific and strong. The myth section takes
the price objection head on. Chi's line, *"She never stopped working with me. We're two years
in, and I'm sitting in my own studio right now"* is the exact antidote to her wound. RLU Pay
with no credit check. The Chelsea results block carries a proper disclaimer.

### Where she closes the tab

**The refund answer is "no," and it is the second-to-last thing on the page.**

> Is my payment refundable? Seats are limited and held for you, so enrollment is final.

Her defining experience is paying and getting nothing. The page's response to her defining
question is that she cannot get out. The guarantee's remedy is more service, not money back.
That may be entirely correct as policy, but the page states it flatly and never acknowledges
what it costs her to read. It does not need to offer refunds. It needs to argue, right there
in that answer, why a guarantee that keeps working is worth more to her than a refund window
she would have to fail to use.

**The guarantee now has a schedule attached to it, and she may not be able to meet it.**

> Complete the curriculum. **Show up to your live sessions.** Run the client strategy.

Live sessions are Tuesdays at noon. If she works, she may structurally fail a condition of
her own guarantee. Recordings protect her learning. They do not obviously protect her
guarantee, because the guarantee says show up. A burned buyer reads every conditional
guarantee as the seller's escape hatch, and this one just grew a timestamp.

**Either watching the replay counts as showing up, or it does not.** Whichever it is, the
page has to say it in the guarantee, not leave it to be discovered in week four.

**Scarcity is vague on the thing she is buying and specific on the bonus.** Lash Cash says
"capped at 44." The certification says "seats are limited" and never gives a number. She has
heard "limited" before. And there is a real number available: six Equipped, four Certified.
That is genuinely scarce and it is checkable. "Four Certified seats" beats "limited" with
precisely the person who does not trust you yet.

**Portal access drops to 12 months for Certified, and only the comparison table says so.**
The Equipped card says 24 months on its face. The Certified card says nothing. So the shorter
term is discovered in row fourteen of a table she has to open. To the woman hunting for the
catch, a term that only appears in the fine print reads as the catch.

---

## Ranked

1. **Resolve the guarantee against the Tuesday noon schedule.** Does the replay count? Say so
   inside the guarantee. Hits all three, worst for the Burned Buyer, and it is a live
   contradiction right now.
2. **Address the working-hours conflict out loud**, somewhere near the schedule.
3. **Give the Stalled Pro a reason for weeks 1 to 4, or route her to Lash Cash.** Right now
   the page argues against its own curriculum.
4. **Move licensing to FAQ two**, and link the state requirements post that is already
   ranking.
5. **Answer the model question** in one sentence where the requirement is stated.
6. **Put the real seat counts on the page.** Four and six are better numbers than "limited."
7. **Put the 12-month access term on the Certified card**, not only in the table.
8. **Decide on 250+, 1,000+ and 9 years.** Documented, or gone.
