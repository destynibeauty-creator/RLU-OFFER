# THE SCENE AI: Ten Buyer Audiences and the Road to $1M

Prepared as a CMO brief, August 26 2026. Written to be argued with.

---

## First, three assumptions I am going to challenge

**Assumption 1: THE SCENE AI is an AI education business.**

The AI education slot at $9/month has an owner. AI Video Bootcamp has roughly
26,500 members, launched around October 2025, and got there on a founder with
586,000 to 650,000 followers built before the doors opened. Competing for the
"teach me AI video" buyer means fighting a competitor with 70x the distribution
in the exact same price slot on the exact same platform.

**The winning position is not teaching AI. It is selling the output of AI to
people who need content and have money.** Hobbyists consume. Businesses buy.

**Assumption 2: $1M can come from the membership.**

$1,000,000 at $9/month requires 9,260 members held for a full year. At $47 it
requires 1,773. From a base of eight members with one confirmed stranger
acquisition, neither is a plan. It is a wish with a spreadsheet attached.

$1M in twelve months has to be a hybrid, and most of it has to come from
buyers paying hundreds or thousands, not nine dollars.

**Assumption 3: The audience should be big.**

The correct audience is the one where your acquisition cost is near zero
because you already have unfair access. You have 9,212 followers and 1,099
engaged email subscribers in one specific industry. That is not a coincidence
to work around. It is the opening move.

## Deep Dive: #1 Beauty and Wellness Business Owners

**Why I picked them.** Every other audience on this list requires you to build
a channel first. This one you already own. 9,212 followers, 1,099 engaged
subscribers, Meta ads that produce leads at $2.56, and nine years of standing
in that industry. Your customer acquisition cost here is close to zero and
nobody else in AI education can say that about any audience.

**Sell them first:** a four-week live cohort. Build your AI brand character,
build your scene library, walk out with thirty days of content.

**The transformation:** she stops being the bottleneck in her own marketing.
Today she cannot post unless she does her hair, sets up a light, and shoots.
After, she generates a month in an afternoon.

**Strongest acquisition channel:** your own email list and Instagram, then Meta
ads against beauty interest stacks with your existing AI creative, which is
already your best performing content.

**Content that stops the scroll:** side by side. Her real studio versus the
generated one. The same face in six settings she never visited. Fifteen seconds
of "this took nine minutes."

**Objections:**
- *"It looks fake and my clients will know."* Show unlabeled work first, then reveal.
- *"I don't have time to learn another thing."* The cohort produces the content while she learns it. She leaves with output, not homework.
- *"Is it wrong to post AI images of my work?"* Real concern in beauty. Answer it directly with a disclosure standard you teach. Never generate fake results. Generate brand and lifestyle imagery.
- *"I can just use the free tools."* She can, and her output will look like everyone else's. Sell the system, not the software.

**Customers needed for $1M:** at $697, 1,435 buyers. Alone, that is not a
million dollar audience in twelve months. **It is the audience that funds and
proves everything above it.**

**Market type:** hybrid. Cohort front end, membership back end.

**Her own inconsistency.** She posts four times in a week, then nothing for
eleven days, then feels behind and posts something she does not like.

2. **A competitor's feed getting noticeably better.**
3. **A rebrand, a new suite, or a move.**
4. **A price increase she is about to announce** and does not feel ready to justify.

# Revision: Medical Aesthetics Moves Up

Recorded August 26 2026, after a competing analysis and two rounds of critique.
**This supersedes the ranking above for audience #6.**

## Why it moves

The original ranking put local professional practices at #6, reasoning that
there was no referral path. That was wrong. **Destyni is an RN. Her buyer is
very often an RN.** A large share of med spas use registered nurses for
injections, and the owner is typically a nurse or nurse practitioner who left
bedside work to open her own place.

That credential is not rapport. It is a service nobody else in the category can
sell.

## Firmographics, verified

| | |
|---|---|
| US med spas | ~10,500, projected 11,553, grown from 8,899 in recent reporting |
| Average annual revenue | $1,398,833 (AmSpa 2024), with 2026 estimates running $1.6M to $2.8M |
| Single-location share | About 81%. **The owner is the buyer. No procurement.** |
| Marketing budget | 8% to 15% of revenue, so $80k to $150k/yr at a $1M clinic |
| Owner profile | Female RN or NP, 35 to 45, suburban, affluent-adjacent metro |

## The positioning

**Not "I'm a nurse, so I get you." That is rapport.**

**"I'm a nurse, so I know what you can't post." That is a service.**

Every agency pitching her can make pretty pictures. None of them can tell her
which pictures draw a board complaint.

## The offer, revised

**Lead with an advertising and brand review plus asset build. $997 to $1,500,
delivered in five days.** The retainer is the upsell for clinics that ask
"can you just do this every month," and she takes only as many as she can
actually deliver.

Why the small first sale wins: fast cash, low delivery load, highest-trust
opening move, and the only version where the license is an asset instead of a
liability.

## Non-negotiables

**No synthetic humans anywhere near a clinical context.** Cut the recurring AI
