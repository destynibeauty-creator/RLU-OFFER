# The pitch

## The change: 20 spec pitches, not 100 cold emails

She has no brand case studies. A rate card from a stranger with 3,838
followers gets deleted. An email that opens with a finished asset made for
that brand does not, because the credential is the work, not the résumé.

So the outbound is: pick a brand, make one piece of content for them before
asking, send it. Twenty of those will outperform a hundred generic emails,
and she can make one in an afternoon.

## Who to hit first, in order

1. **Her own vendors.** She sources product for Rich Lash. Adhesive
   manufacturers, packaging, tools, fulfillment. They already know her name,
   already have her invoices, and most of them have terrible content. This is
   warm outbound disguised as cold.
2. **Hair and wig brands.** Enormous ad spend, huge Black beauty market,
   content that mostly looks the same. A consistent character is worth more
   to them than to almost anyone.
3. **Lash and beauty brands** she does not buy from. She speaks the category
   fluently and can name what is wrong with their creative in one line.
4. **Nail, skincare, body care.** Same shape.
5. **Atlanta businesses with real budgets.** Restaurants, dispensaries,
   dealerships, med spas. Local closes faster and pays faster.

Find the brand marketing lead or head of content on LinkedIn, or the
marketing@ address. Not the brand's Instagram DMs. That inbox is for fans.

## The email

Subject: `Made this for [BRAND] before I asked`

> Hi [NAME],
>
> I made this for [BRAND] before reaching out, so you can see the work instead
> of reading about it.
>
> [LINK]
>
> I'm Destyni Triplett. I direct AI campaigns for beauty brands. Same footage
> a shoot gives you, without the studio, the talent booking, the location or
> the six weeks. The part most people cannot do is hold the same character
> across a whole campaign. That's what I build.
>
> [ONE LINE ABOUT THEIR BRAND. Something specific you noticed. Not flattery.]
>
> If it's useful, my rates are here: [LINK]. If it isn't, delete this and I
> won't follow up twice.
>
> Destyni Triplett, RN
> AI Creative Director
> @profashionalist

## Rules for it

- One follow-up, seven days later, one line. Then stop. The line is:
  "Bumping this once in case it got buried. No hard feelings either way."
- Never send the rate card first. Always the work first.
- Never promise them sales, revenue, engagement or ROI. Sell cost, speed and
  control, which are the three things that are actually true and verifiable.
- Do not apologize for the follower count, and do not bring it up. They are
  buying production, not audience. If they ask, the answer is that her
  audience is not the product, the work is, and here is the work.
- Every reply gets a call. The call closes, not the email.

## The one-pager she needs

Three pieces of work, what she makes, what it costs, how to book. One page.
Not a website. A link that loads in three seconds on a phone.
