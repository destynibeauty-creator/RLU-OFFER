## 1. What the business is

Destyni directs AI campaigns for brands. Not a tool, not a course, not an
agency. One director who makes the video, the short cuts and the photos a
brand would otherwise get from a photo shoot.

Two other things sit around it and are not this business:
Profashionalist is the attention. THE SCENE Campus at $9 is the front door
and stays $9. Rich Lash University is separate and stays women.

## 2. Who buys it

**The brand buyer.** Brand manager, creative director, CMO at a smaller
company. They buy a launch, a campaign, a spokesperson. They judge on whether
it feels right and looks expensive. Budget comes from a campaign line
approved quarterly. They are slow, which is the trade.

**Not the performance buyer.** Growth leads and media buyers want thirty
disposable variants a month and judge on cost per sale. Character
consistency, which is the whole moat, is worthless to them. They want
variation. Add a volume tier only after one client's numbers exist to point
at.

## 3. Why they buy from her and not somebody cheaper

**One. Most AI still gets us wrong.** Plastic skin, hair no real person has,
beards and waves the model guessed at, clothes nobody would wear. A Black
customer sees it in half a second and says so in the comments, which turns a
creative problem into something the brand has to answer for in public.
Destyni has been on the receiving end of that comment section herself. Nine
years in Black beauty on real faces before she ever touched one of these
tools. Him and her, not just her.

**Two. The same person stays the same person.** Most AI drifts by the second
asset. Holding one face, body, hair and voice across a hundred pieces over a
year is the hard part and it is what the top package sells.

**Three. A spokesperson who never becomes a problem.** Does not age out of
the campaign, does not double book, does not post something at 2am, does not
turn up on a competitor's packaging, does not come back next year with an
agent and a new number. For a brand that got burned by an influencer, this
alone is the sale.

## 5. How she gets in front of them

**Twenty spec pitches, not a hundred cold emails.** She has no brand case
studies, so a rate card from a stranger with 3,838 followers gets deleted.
Make one finished piece for the brand before asking, and lead the email with
it. The work is the credential.

**Who first:** her own product vendors, who already know her name and have
her invoices. Then hair and wig brands. Then lash and beauty. Then grooming,
barbershops, menswear, sneakers, spirits. Then Atlanta businesses with real
budgets, which close and pay faster.

Marketing lead on LinkedIn or the marketing@ address. Never the brand's
Instagram DMs.

**Rules:** work before rate card, always. One follow-up seven days later,
one line, then stop. Never promise sales, revenue or return. Sell cost,
speed and control, which are true and checkable. Do not bring up the follower
count and do not apologize for it, because they are buying production, not
audience. Every reply gets a call.

## 6. What is deliberately not on the card

**Websites and running ads.** She can do both and does for herself. Adding
them turns a specialist into a small agency, changes the comparison from a
photo shoot to a freelancer, and drags her into being judged on a cost per
sale she does not control. It belongs in one credibility sentence, and as an
upsell after the first project, never before it.

**The education ladder above $9.** At 13,000 combined followers, a hundred
buyers at $2,997 would be nearly one percent of the entire audience. It is a
2027 engine. Build it, do not bank on it.

## 7. What is missing

**Proof.** The card has none. Good claims, nothing behind them. First real
client, a before and after goes on the page and it stops being a claim.

**Distribution.** Nothing has broken out yet. 19.6K views a month on
Profashionalist, 28.5K on RichDestyni. Instagram mostly serves existing
followers, so TikTok is the cheap shot at strangers using the library that
already exists. Outbound runs in parallel because it is the one engine that
does not need an audience.

## 8. The math, honestly

$60,000 collected in 2026 so far. $1M total means $940,000 in four months
against a business running near $7,500 a month.

At these prices $940,000 is about 45 transactions: 22 spokesperson packages,
14 campaigns, and nine retainer months. Correcting the prices to market moved
the number further away, not closer, which is the real cost of having priced
it backwards the first time.

Money has to be collected by Dec 31 and brand contracts pay net 30 to net 60,
so the real signing window closes around mid-November. Ten weeks, not
seventeen.

**Checkpoint: October 15.** Two signed campaigns by then or the million is
off and the same machine gets pointed at the best quarter she has ever had.
