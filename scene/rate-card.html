Most AI still
gets us wrong.

Skin that looks like plastic. Hair no real person has. Beards and waves the computer clearly guessed at. Clothes nobody would actually put on. If you sell to Black customers, they see it in half a second.

And when they see it, they say so in your comments. Loudly. That stops being a marketing problem and turns into the kind you have to answer for in public. I have been on the wrong end of that comment section myself. It is exactly why I work the way I do.

I make the other kind. Real skin with real texture. Hair that is actually their hair. Him and her, and everybody else in the shot.

The second thing: I keep the same person looking like the same person. Most AI cannot. By the second video the face has shifted and the body has changed, and what you paid for stops looking like a campaign and starts looking like a mistake.

You already know what a photo shoot costs. The studio, booking the model, the location, hair and makeup, the editing, and the six weeks before you see anything you can use. None of that happens here.

Questions people ask

Before they ask me

Why does the skin and hair thing keep coming up?

Because most of these tools were not built with us in mind, and the miss is obvious to the exact customer you are trying to reach. Skin, hair, beards, edges, waves. The small things that tell somebody a brand actually sees them. Getting it right takes direction, real reference, and knowing what the finish is supposed to look like. I spent nine years in Black beauty, on real faces, before I ever touched one of these tools.

Can you make a double of our founder?

Yes, and it is usually the easier one to get approved internally, because that face is already the brand. It needs photos and video of them from several angles and their signed permission, no exceptions. Start with the Test Run if you want to see the likeness before committing. I run my own double, so this is not theory for me.

Will people know it is AI?

Some will. I label the work as AI where the platform or the campaign calls for it, and I will not build you something pretending a real person endorsed your product. Being upfront has not hurt the work. Pretending would.

What if they look different in the next batch?

That is how nearly all AI content falls apart and it is the thing I build against. On the spokesperson package you own the written guide, so they stay the same even if you take the work somewhere else.

Can we run it as paid ads?

Yes. Every package covers paid social and the files come sized for it. TV and billboards need different permissions, so those get priced separately.

Who owns it?

You own everything I deliver, on every package. On the spokesperson package you also own the person, files and all. I keep the right to show the work in my portfolio unless you would rather I did not, which is a normal thing to ask and costs nothing.

Why trust a lash educator with our campaign?

Because I have run a product company for nine years, trained over 250 women, and shipped to real customers, so I know what a campaign has to do for a business. I am a Registered Nurse, which is where the discipline comes from. And you saw the work before you read a word of this, which is the whole point.
