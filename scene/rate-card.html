Most AI still
gets us wrong.

Skin that goes plastic. Hair texture the model has clearly never seen. Beards and waves it cannot draw. Styling nobody would actually wear. If you sell to Black customers, they clock it in half a second.

And when they clock it, the comments do the work for you. Not the good kind. That stops being a creative problem and becomes a brand safety problem, and it lands on the same desk as everything else that goes wrong in public. I have been on the receiving end of that comment section myself, which is exactly why I direct the way I do.

I build the other thing. Rich skin tones with real texture, hair that is actually their hair, styling and detail that reads right. Him and her, and everybody in the frame behind them. That is not a filter applied at the end. It is the whole way the work is directed.

The second thing most people cannot do: hold one character across an entire campaign. Same face, same body, same voice, same world, in January and in June. Most AI content falls apart by the second asset. That consistency is the product, and it is the reason the top tier on this card exists.

You already know what a production day costs you. The studio, the talent booking, the location fee, the glam team, the retouching, and the six weeks between the brief and the first usable cut. This skips all of it.

Asked and answered

The five that come up

Why does the skin and hair part keep coming up?

Because most models were not trained to render us well, and the failure is obvious to the exact customer you are trying to reach. Skin, hair, beards, edges, waves, the details that tell somebody the brand actually sees them. Getting it right takes direction, reference and someone who knows what the finish is supposed to look like. I spent nine years in Black beauty, on real faces, before I ever touched a model.

Can you build a twin of our founder?

Yes, and it is often the easier sell internally, because the face is already the brand. It needs a reference capture and a signed likeness release from that person, no exceptions. Start with a Pilot if you want to see the likeness before committing to the full build. I run my own twin, so this is not theoretical for me.

Will people know it is AI?

Some will. The work is labeled as AI where the platform or the campaign calls for it, and I will not build you something that pretends a real person endorsed your product. Disclosure has not hurt the work. Pretending would.

What if the character changes in the next batch?

That is the failure mode of nearly all AI content and it is the specific thing I build against. On the Character tier you own the bible, so consistency survives even if you take the work elsewhere.

Can we use it in paid?

Yes. Every tier includes paid social usage, and the delivery spec is cut for it. Broadcast and out of home are quoted separately because the licensing is different.

Who owns the output?

You own the delivered assets on every tier. On the Character tier you also own the character itself, files and all. I keep the right to show the work in my portfolio unless you ask me not to, which is a normal thing to ask and costs nothing.

Why trust a lash educator with our campaign?

Because I have run a product company for nine years, trained over 250 women, and shipped to real customers, so I know what a campaign has to do for a business. I am a Registered Nurse, which is where the discipline comes from. And the work speaks before I do, which is why you got a finished asset before you got this page.
