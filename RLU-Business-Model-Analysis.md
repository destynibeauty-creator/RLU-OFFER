**Verdict: build toward $10,000/month for the next 12 months, without apology.** Not as a consolation prize — because $10K/month at 20 hrs/week *is* your stated success condition (presence, time autonomy), and $91K/month at 60 hrs/week is your stated failure condition. ## 3. Unit economics: should Equipped survive?

The margin identity ($1,622 = $1,622) hides the real comparison. Load in everything:

| | Certified | Equipped |
|---|---|---|
| Gross margin (challenge price) | $1,622 | $1,622 |
| Margin % | 81% | 54% |
| Cash you front | $375 | $1,375 |
| Deposit covers COGS? | Yes ($750 > $375) | Barely ($1,200 vs $1,375 — **you're $175 out of pocket at deposit**) |
| Incremental founder time | 0 | ~1.5 hrs of 1:1s [ASSUMPTION: 2 × 45 min] + shipping/logistics |
| Margin per incremental founder-hour | ∞ (no incremental time) | $1,622 ÷ 1.5 ≈ $1,081/hr — still excellent, but finite |
| Payment rails | Full BNPL menu works | **Affirm/Sezzle cap below $2,997 → pay-in-full effectively broken; RLU Pay only** → you carry more lending risk on exactly the tier where you front more cash |
| Refund/default exposure | $375 of stuff shipped | $1,375 shipped, possibly unrecoverable |

So Equipped is strictly worse on every operational dimension and identical on margin dollars. The case *against* killing it is a single data point, but it's your most important one: **Alexandria — your only organic buyer — chose Equipped unprompted.** The studio is doing something the margin math can't see: it makes the outcome tangible ("I will have a functioning studio"), it self-selects serious buyers, and it justifies the price to a skeptical buyer better than curriculum ever will. The prior audit reached the same conclusion about the shipped-studio being one of your four hardest-to-copy differentiators.

**Recommendation: keep both tiers, but stop selling the studio at cost while donating your 1:1 time.** Three moves, in order of preference:

1. **Raise Equipped by $300** ($3,297 challenge / $4,297 regular). The premium now carries $300 toward your 1:1 time (~$200/session at 2 sessions). Your buyer is not price-comparing at the $300 level between tiers; she's deciding tangible vs. not. Note this also moves Equipped further from the Affirm/Sezzle caps, which were already broken — so nothing is lost on rails.
2. **Raise the Equipped deposit to $1,500** so the deposit covers COGS plus shipping. You should never be cash-negative on an enrollment day one; you're the lender, not the borrower.
3. **Define the 1:1 allotment explicitly** (2 sessions, use-by dates). Unbounded "1:1 access" is the only element of your delivery model that scales linearly with heads, and it's on the tier where you make no extra margin.

Do **not** add tiers, and do not build a "studio-only" upsell for Certified — that recreates the Equipped logistics with none of the commitment signal.

## 5. The campus as primary engine: the $60/day ad

### The operating rules you asked for

- **Break-even CPPM: ~$56 on subscriptions alone; ~$100 with full ladder EV.** Because the ladder EV is unproven, treat **$50 as your working ceiling**.
- **Scale to $100/day when:** CPPM ≤ **$30** across at least $1,000 of spend, AND ≥85% of ad-acquired members are still paying at day 30. (The second condition catches the failure mode where ads buy tourists.)
- **Turn it off and fix the offer when:** CPPM > **$60** after $1,000 of spend, OR first-month churn of ad-acquired members > 25%. Both mean the problem is the landing page / first-week experience, not the ad — more spend just buys the same leak faster. Fix, then re-test with $500.
- Judge nothing before $1,000 of spend (~17 days). At $60/day the noise at day 5 will whipsaw you.

### Projected campus size and MRR

Members(t) = adds × (1 − 0.85^t) ÷ 0.15, at 15% monthly churn, plus your existing ~15:

| CPPM → adds/mo | Month 3 | Month 6 | Month 12 | MRR @ M12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $15 → 122 | ~320 | ~510 | ~705 | ~$6,350 |
| **$30 → 61 (base)** | **~165** | **~260** | **~355** | **~$3,190** |
| $50 → 36 | ~105 | ~160 | ~215 | ~$1,940 |

Steady state at base case = 61 ÷ 0.15 ≈ 405 members. One sober framing you should hold onto: at base case, **cumulative year-one subscription revenue (~$25,700 gross) roughly equals cumulative ad spend ($21,900).** The campus ad engine approximately *washes* on subscriptions in year one. Its actual return is (a) the cert and bridge sales it incubates and (b) a compounding owned audience — which is exactly what the Section 1 model says the business runs on. ### Campus → certification: rate and lag

Comparable low-ticket-community → high-ticket funnels run **1–3% of members per year**, with a lag of **3–12 months** — and your own data sits at the slow end. Concretely: a 350-member campus at 2%/year ≈ **7 certs/year ≈ 0.6/month arriving passively by month 12** — and the first ad-acquired cert conversions shouldn't be *expected* before month 6. Passive conversion alone will not fill cohorts; that's what the DM system (Section 7) and the bridge (Section 6) are for — both exist to compress the Alexandria timeline from 12+ months toward 3–6.

**Verdict: run it, starting Monday as planned, at $60/day, with the kill/scale rules above written down before the first dollar spends.**

Zero made the jump. Alexandria needed 12+ months of free incubation to make it. The bridge's job is to compress that incubation.

### Design: the Lash Brand Sprint (build the thing already on your roadmap)

- **Format: 7–10 day self-paced sprint, fully recorded**, with templates: brand name + trademark basics (you own this territory — Rich Lash is federally registered, teach from that scar tissue), IG profile rebuild, service menu and pricing builder, booking setup, first-content calendar. Outcome: "your lash brand exists in public by day 10."

Different job, zero curriculum overlap, so it cannot substitute for the certification — it *creates demand* for it, because a woman with a named brand and a service menu now urgently needs the skill and the credential to be legitimate. (The prior audit scored a self-paced *technique* product 8/10 cannibalization risk and said don't build it. It was right. This is the opposite product.)
- **Your live load: zero incremental.** Questions route to one monthly office-hours slot folded into an existing campus call. Build cost: [ASSUMPTION] 30–40 hours once, which is why it's scheduled in the plan's months 2–3, not week 1.
- **Ladder position:** Challenge/ads → $9 Campus → **$297 Sprint** → $750/$1,200 deposit → Certification. Each step is now 3–33x the last instead of one 83x cliff. Offer it at day ~14 of campus membership (after first-win onboarding), and as the down-sell in every DM thread that stalls on the deposit.

### Revenue contribution, modeled

[ASSUMPTION] 10% of new paying members buy within 60 days (they've already paid once and are in the high-intent window; range 5–15%).

- Base case (61 adds/mo): ~6 sales/mo × $297 ≈ **$1,800/month, ~95% margin** once built.
- Ascension: [ASSUMPTION] 20–30% of Sprint buyers use the credit within 6 months → ~1.5 additional certs/month by month 9+ — worth more than the Sprint revenue itself (~$2,400/month margin) and the main reason to build it.

### The dilution question, addressed directly

You asked whether certifying other instructors dilutes the credential whose core trust signal is "a Registered Nurse scored the work." Answer: **it dilutes the credential only if you delegate the scoring. It does not dilute it if you delegate the teaching.**

You already know this model — it's how your own profession works. Clinical instructors teach nursing students; the board certifies them. Nobody thinks an RN license is diluted because the person who taught med-surg wasn't the state board. The trust signal was never "Destyni personally delivered every lecture." It's "an RN's standard decided whether this woman passes."

So the rule, adoptable now and durable at any scale: **instruction may scale; certification never leaves your signature.** Every certificate is scored against your rubric and signed "Destyni Triplett, RN" after you (not a delegate) review the final portfolio. Put that sentence on the enroll page the day you onboard your first co-teacher. Reviewing final portfolios is hours per cohort, not per student-week — it's the one duty that doesn't need to scale away, because it *is* the product.

Two sequencing notes: (a) hiring graduates as success *coaches* (hire #2) carries zero dilution risk — coaching isn't certifying — and actually strengthens the brand ("taught by women who did it, certified by the RN"). (b) A paid instructor-certification program ("train the trainer") is a real future revenue line, but at 2 enrollments YTD it's a distraction; park it behind the hire-#4 trigger.
