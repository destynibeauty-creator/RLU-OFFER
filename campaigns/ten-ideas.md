# Ten for The Retention Room

Built against what the account already proves. Her twins pull, her talking
head in scrubs does not, bare branded cards do not. So every one of these is
visually impossible, emotionally specific, or both. None of them is a woman
standing in front of a neon sign explaining something.

Each has a different job. Do not make ten of the same post.

---

## 1. The Nine Days
**Job: the share.**

> Nobody pulled anything. It let go on its own.

Extreme macro. A bond between two dark fibres. Oil creeps along the lash,
reaches the bond, the polymer clouds, softens, releases. The extension slides
away untouched.

This is the one nobody in the category has. It cannot be filmed. It is her
entire thesis in nine seconds and it is the reason to build the campaign at
all.

---

## 2. Certified But Not Confident
**Job: the DM.**

> You have the certificate. Nobody ever told you why they fall out.

A framed lash certificate on a wall, slightly crooked. Pull back to a woman
sitting alone in an empty lash room at night, phone face up on the bed, one
message glowing on it. She does not pick it up.

This is the number one objection in the entire category and it is the one
piece here that will make women message her instead of just watching.

---

## 3. The Shower
**Job: the save.**

> This is where your set actually died. Not in your chair.

Steam. Hot water running down a face. Cleanser foaming, oil in it. Extreme
macro as the water hits the lash line and the film of oil creeps in behind it.
Beautiful and slightly horrifying.

The villain of the whole story, and nobody films it because it happens in
somebody's bathroom three days after they leave.

---

## 4. Two Rooms
**Job: the save.**

> Same artist. Same adhesive. Same technique. One of these lasted.

Split screen. Two identical lash rooms, two identical sets being applied.
Gauges in the corner of each: 30% and 55%. Cut to day nine. One set intact.
One shedding.

Makes the invisible variable visible. This is the piece that makes a woman go
buy a hygrometer, which means she thinks about you every day after.

---

## 5. POV: You Are The Adhesive
**Job: the novelty share.**

> POV: you are the drop of glue holding her whole set together.

First person, camera inside the bond. Tweezers approaching from above like a
crane. The fibre lowering in. The world going still as the cure sets. Then,
later, warmth and water and the grip going.

Strange, immersive, and completely unlike anything in a lash feed. This is the
one that gets stitched.

---

## 6. The Autopsy
**Job: the follow.**

> Case 01. Nine days. She swore she wasn't using oil.

Set photo, HUD panel over it: adhesive, temp, humidity, days lasted. Her voice
over the top naming the cause in thirty seconds.

Number them from the start. A numbered series is the only format here that
makes someone go looking for the ones they missed, and that is what a follow
is.

**Use only her own sets or ones she has permission for.** This format works
because it is honest.

---

## 7. The Cure, In Pink
**Job: the signature.**

> Two seconds decides nine days.

Extreme macro, one droplet hardening from the outside in, moisture visible in
the air. Lit entirely in #F600A2.

The category is about to be flooded with purple UV curing content. Pink is
already her colour. Whoever owns a colour in a feed gets recognised before
they get read.

---

## 8. Twin Vlog: A Day In The Lab
**Job: watch time.**

> Come diagnose sets with me.

Handheld, arm's length, her walking through the Lab talking to camera. Coffee.
A screen. A photo pulled up. Natural, unposed, expensive.

This is the format that already works on her account. The only change is the
room she is standing in.

---

## 9. The Lash Cycle
**Job: the save, and the beginner.**

> Half of what you think is bad retention is just her natural cycle.

Clean animated cross section. One natural lash growing, resting, shedding, and
a new one starting. Gold line work, pink highlight on the active stage.

Highest save rate format in the niche and the single most useful thing a
beginner can learn. It also makes her look like the only honest person in the
category, because she is telling them not everything is their fault.

---

## 10. The Chair
**Job: the emotional close.**

> She was going to be your client for two years.

One lash bed, locked camera, time lapse. The same woman returning again and
again, seasons changing through the window behind her. Then the reverse: the
chair, empty, staying empty.

No numbers, no money, no promise. Just what a client who comes back actually
looks like over time, and what she looks like when she does not.

---

## How to run them

**Make 1, 2 and 6 first.** The mechanism, the objection and the format. If
there is time for nothing else, those three are the campaign.

**One caption pattern, every time.** One line on what you just saw. One line
on what it means. One line to the Room. Never a paragraph.

**Every caption ends the same way:** Tuesday, September 1, 8 PM ET. Link in
bio.

**Label the generated ones as generated.** It costs nothing and it protects
the screenshots on the sales page.

**Whichever one is pulling by Friday goes into the ad set for the weekend.**
That is a real ad, made from a post that already proved it can hold a thumb.

---

## Rules

No income or earnings claim in any of these, including number 10, which is why
it counts appointments in pictures and never in dollars.

Her vocabulary. Shedding, falling out too soon, didn't last, poor retention.
Never hold, never "sets fail". See BRAND-VOICE.md.

Palette on every prompt: near black #0A0A0A, hot pink #F600A2, gold #D4AF37.
No brown, no beige, no warm wood.

Type in CapCut, never in the generation.
